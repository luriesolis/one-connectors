Getting started

What is a connector?

A connector links One, Factorial’s AI agent, to another app your company uses, like Slack, Jira or HubSpot. You can then find information and complete tasks across those tools from the One chat, without switching applications.

Connectors use MCP (Model Context Protocol), an open standard that lets AI assistants like One communicate with external applications and services. For example, you could ask One to:

  • Find information from a project in Jira.
  • Summarize Slack conversations you have access to.
  • Retrieve customer information from your connected CRM.
  • Perform supported actions across connected tools, subject to your permissions.
Who sets it up?

A Factorial admin installs the connector for the company from the Connectors section. Then each person connects their own account with one click.

Anyone on the team can browse the catalog and request a connector. Their admin is notified and can install it.

What do I need to use connectors?

Connectors are available to every company with Factorial One enabled. You also need an account in the app you want to connect.

What do “In progress” and “Coming soon” mean?
  • Live: available to connect today.
  • In progress: actively being built. Google (Gmail, Calendar, Drive) and Microsoft (Teams, Calendar, 365 Copilot) are next in line.
  • Coming soon: on the roadmap. Your Factorial contact can share the latest timing.
Our tool isn’t listed. Can we request it?

Yes. More than 500 apps are on the roadmap, and customer demand helps decide what comes next. Share the tools your team relies on with your Factorial contact.

We built our own internal tool or MCP server. Can One connect to it?

Not yet, but it’s on our roadmap for Q4 2026. Many companies now run their own internal tools, data platforms or AI agents. Soon you’ll be able to connect your own MCP server to One, so the answers One gives can combine Factorial with the systems your team built in-house.

Until then, One connects to the official, verified connectors in the catalog above. Your Factorial contact can share the latest timing.

Security, privacy and permissions

One respects your existing permissions. Connecting a new tool does not give One unrestricted access to your company’s data.

What information can a connector access?

Only information that the person who connected it is authorized to access. Two permission boundaries apply:

  • Factorial permissions: One respects the access permissions configured in Factorial.
  • External app permissions: each connector works with the permissions of the person who signed in to it.

For example, if you connect your Slack account, One can’t access private channels or conversations your Slack account can’t view. Information you’re authorized to access and share through One may be sent to the connected service when you use it.

Does connecting a tool give One extra permissions?

No. Connecting a tool doesn’t grant One additional permissions in Factorial or in the external app. One works within the existing access controls of both. If you can’t view something or perform an action yourself, connecting a tool doesn’t override that.

Can a connector access other people’s information?

Not automatically. Each connector signs in as the person who connected it and works within that person’s permissions.

  • Someone connecting their CRM account only sees the CRM data their account can retrieve.
  • A manager may see more, based on their existing role.
  • Nobody gains access to restricted information just by asking One.
Does connecting my account give others access to it?

No. Connecting an app with your own credentials doesn’t give anyone else access to that account. Each person signs in with their own credentials and permissions, unless a connector has been specifically set up to use a shared or service account.

Is our company’s data shared with the connected service?

When you use a connector, the information needed to fulfil your request is exchanged between One and that service. For example, if you ask One to create a task in a project management tool, the details of that task are sent to it.

  • Connecting a service doesn’t transfer all your company’s data.
  • What’s exchanged depends on the connector’s capabilities and the actions you take.
  • The external service’s own data handling and retention policies apply.

Connect only services you trust, and review their privacy and security policies.

Where is data processed? Can it leave the EU?

Factorial’s connector infrastructure is hosted in the European Union. Data residency also depends on the external services you connect: if a service is hosted outside the EU, information shared with it may be processed outside the EU.

Review each provider’s hosting location, privacy policy and data processing terms before enabling a connection.

Can One make changes or act on my behalf?

Some connectors are read-only. Others support actions such as creating or updating records. What’s available depends on the connector and on the permissions the external service grants you. One can only take an action when you have permission to take it yourself, and every action is traceable to the person who asked. Each card in the catalog above describes what One can do with that app.

Are connectors secure and reliable?

We only offer official, verified connectors that are reviewed against our security requirements before they’re made available in One. We don’t support unverified or unofficial MCP servers.

  • Access control: connectors can’t grant access beyond what the connected person already has.
  • Reliability: we prioritize trusted integrations for a stable experience.
  • Transparency: you stay in control of which services you connect to One.
Who is responsible for the security of external services?

Security is shared:

  • Factorial and One enforce their own access controls and permissions.
  • External providers are responsible for the security, infrastructure and data handling of their services.
  • Your organization chooses trusted providers, reviews their permissions and makes sure use follows internal security and compliance policies.
What are the best practices for using connectors securely?
  • Connect trusted services from reputable providers.
  • Review permissions: know what information and actions each connector can reach.
  • Apply least-privilege access: grant only what the use case needs.
  • Check data residency: see where providers host and process information.
  • Follow company policies for security and privacy.
  • Review connections regularly and remove the ones you no longer need.
